# Stock-aware linking for adaptive clearing

FreeCAD Adaptive2d 1.1.0 at commit `34a9716668b1ddeb55b914f1c5be644826bdbbbf` buffers a proposed tool-center link by cutter radius and safety clearance, subtracts the cleared region, and checks the remainder. Its solver can search for a detour and can test a short connector against a cutting-area target. If linking cannot be established, it classifies the move for retraction. [Pinned FreeCAD Adaptive2d source](https://github.com/FreeCAD/FreeCAD/blob/34a9716668b1ddeb55b914f1c5be644826bdbbbf/src/Mod/CAM/libarea/Adaptive.cpp#L2132)

Whittle's **Stock-aware linking** checkbox is available in Adaptive clearing and persists in `.cam`. Existing operations retain clearance linking by default. At each depth, the planner builds a cleared-region model from the cutter sweeps of completed cutting paths. It tests the whole direct connector sweep against that region, including the cutter at the endpoints. On a closed loop, it also tries a bounded set of nearby start points. The link footprint is expanded and the cleared-region approximation is inset to account for polygon and integer-grid tolerances.

A proven-clear connector runs at feed. A short connector of at most one cutter radius may also stay down as a cutting feed when its total fresh swept area fits the radial-engagement reference and its cutter envelope clears the analytic sketch walls and islands. The preview reports total stay-down links and identifies how many are short cutting links. When no candidate passes, Whittle uses its existing clearance retract, rapid traverse, and plunge or helical entry. Every new depth starts with its configured entry.

The planner uses TypeScript and Whittle's existing Clipper2 dependency and neutral motions. This version considers direct connectors and the completed cuts in the current operation at the current depth. See [routed stay-down links](CAM_ADAPTIVE_DETOURS.md) for the bounded detour search. Earlier-operation stock and full shank, holder, and fixture collision checks remain outside the linking model.
